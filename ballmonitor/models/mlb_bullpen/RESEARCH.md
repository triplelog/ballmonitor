# Research: mlb_bullpen walk-forward study

_Generated by `python -m ballmonitor.models.mlb_bullpen.research`; do not edit by hand._

Data: MLB Stats API box scores, play-by-play, active rosters and transactions (`fetch.py`). **Train** = 2025 regular season, 4372 team-games (2025-04-15 to 2025-09-28, 30 teams). **Test** = 2026 regular season, 4858 team-games from 2429 games (2026-03-25 to 2026-09-27, 30 teams). Every fitted number in the season-forward results uses train only; the test season is scored once. The walk-forward rows refit the fitted parts before each 2026 month on all earlier games (2025 + earlier 2026 months; the simulator's choice weights and hook settings stay at their 2025 fit), giving 7 monthly test splits. A team-game is one case; cases within a team share a bullpen, so the effective sample is smaller than the count: standard errors are shown naive and, in brackets, clustered by team (30 clusters).

Every input is pre-game information: workloads from earlier games, usage history and inferred roles from earlier roster-games, starter stamina from earlier starts, posted lineups, the published schedule, the game-day active roster, and team / league usage from earlier dates.

## 2. The four improvements

### 2.1 Calibration

Logistic recalibration of the simulated appearance frequency, fitted on 2025 (where the simulator is partly in-sample: its choice weights were fitted on the same season's calls): logit P = calib_intercept_<role> + calib_slope × logit P_sim.

* calibration alone (v0.4.0 simulator): `calib_intercept_closer` +0.001 ± 0.034, `calib_intercept_setup` -0.086 ± 0.018, `calib_intercept_middle` -0.064 ± 0.019, `calib_intercept_long` -0.198 ± 0.041, `calib_slope` +0.921 ± 0.016.
* with 2a + 2b (simulator after the engine refit, with tendency): `calib_intercept_closer` +0.048 ± 0.034, `calib_intercept_setup` -0.023 ± 0.019, `calib_intercept_middle` +0.002 ± 0.019, `calib_intercept_long` -0.130 ± 0.041, `calib_slope` +0.931 ± 0.016, `tendency_weight` +0.881 ± 0.319.
* First reliever: a temperature on the simulated first-out distribution was fitted as weight 0.992 ± 0.028 on ln P_sim (1 = no change), so the simulator's first-reliever distribution is already calibrated in spread; only the blend with the direct model (2.4) improves it.

### 2.2 Bullpen size: engine refit and team / league tendency

**Diagnosis.** The v0.4.0 hook settings were tuned on every 2nd train row; rows alternate home / away, so that sample was 100% home teams (2186 team-games, 3.42 relievers per game). Home teams use more relievers than away teams (they always pitch the 9th; the away team skips the bottom of the 9th when trailing), so the target was too high: the 2025 average is 3.26 pool relievers per team-game, 2026 3.24. The 3.43 vs 3.24 gap reported for v0.4.0 was this tuning bias, not league drift.

**Engine refit.** Same grid search, now on a home/away-balanced train sample (both teams of every even game_pk, 2186 team-games × 40 simulated games), target = pool relievers per game (3.25) and starter outs (15.6).

### 2.3 Active roster

**Finding: the pool is already the game-day active roster.** It is built from the box score's roster (the 26-man roster at game time), so arms on the IL, optioned or DFA'd before the game are never in it: 0 / 0 inactive pool reliever-games in 2025 / 2026, i.e. no prediction mass sits on inactive arms and a roster filter cannot change the backtest. To check, the MLB Stats API active roster was fetched for every team on every game date and the day before (11k cached requests).

* The API roster for a date is an **end-of-day** state: nearly every pool arm it misses pitched that day and was optioned / put on the IL afterwards. Filtering on it would leak post-game moves (and remove arms who pitched), so it is not an input.
* Same-day moves matter for live use: on about a fifth of team-games a pitcher left the roster since yesterday. Build the pool from the day's announced roster (or mark departed arms `active = false`); a pool built from yesterday's roster would carry those arms.
* What the roster data adds as a pre-game signal is `new_arrival` (joined today), a feature of both direct models; its value is the difference between the two direct-model rows in section 1.
* Arms placed on the IL in the next days (unknowable before the game) are a small source of error.

### 2.4 Direct model and blend
