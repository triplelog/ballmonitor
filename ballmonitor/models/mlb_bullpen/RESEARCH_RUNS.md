# Research: bullpen run prevention (mlb_bullpen)

_Generated by `python -m ballmonitor.models.mlb_bullpen.research_runs`; do not edit by hand._

**Question.** Can the simulated bullpen usage tell us how many runs more or fewer than usual a
team's relievers should allow today, and does that number predict anything out of sample?

Data: MLB Stats API box scores and play-by-play (`fetch.py`). **Train** = 2025 regular season (4372 team-games from 2025-04-15), **test** = 2026 (4858 team-games from 2429 games, 30 teams). Every fitted number in the season-forward rows uses 2025 only; the walk-forward rows refit the linear models before each of the 7 2026 months on all earlier games. Standard errors are shown naive and, in brackets, clustered by team (30 clusters): a team's games share a bullpen, so the effective sample is far smaller than the case count.

## 1. Metric

* **Runs on the mound**: runs that scored during plate appearances a pitcher pitched (play-by-play score changes). Inherited runners count against the pitcher on the mound. The cached box scores carry no per-pitcher R / ER / H / BB / K / HR, so an earned-run or FIP-style rate is not possible without re-downloading every box score.
* **Reliever rating** (runs per out) = (runs + k × m) / (outs + k) over all his EARLIER relief outings in the data (any team, from 2025 opening day). No history → m.
* k and m fitted on 2025 (16010 relief appearances, 713 pitchers): at the start of each month from May, every reliever is rated from his earlier 2025 relief outings and the rating predicts his runs per out over that month; loss = Σ (runs − rating × outs)² / outs over 1954 pitcher-months (a month, not single outings, because an outing's length depends on how it went). **k = 300 pseudo-outs, m = 0.190 runs/out**. League reliever rate 2025 = 0.1841 runs/out (4.97 per 9 innings). Relievers with no earlier 2025 relief outing ran at 0.190 runs/out (335 pitcher-months, 4241 outs), hence m slightly above the league rate (unknown arms lean replacement-level). Loss: shrunk rating 1133.4, league-average constant 1139.2, unshrunk own rate 1783.7: individual run prevention is mostly noise, hence the heavy shrinkage (300 outs ≈ 100 innings).

* **Expected outs per reliever**: `reported` (chosen on 2025: R² of bullpen runs on workload + vs-average = 0.0176 with P(appear) × simulated outs per appearance (`reported`) vs 0.0105 with simulated outs per game (`simulated`)). Usage comes from the shipped v0.5 model (blend probabilities, 500 simulations).
* **Bullpen runs vs average** = Σ xOuts_i × (rating_i − league rate): positive = this bullpen, as it is expected to be used today, should allow more runs than a league-average bullpen pitching the same expected outs.
* **Bullpen runs vs usual** = Σ xOuts_i × rating_i − X × usual rate, usual rate = ratings weighted by each arm's share of the pool's relief outs over his last 30 roster-games: the availability / usage-mix effect alone. A second version (**vs rested**) compares with the same pool simulated fully rested (all `pitches_days` = 0).
* Benchmarks (all pre-game): team season-to-date bullpen runs/out shrunk with 3840 pseudo-outs toward the league rate, × expected bullpen outs (**naive**); starter season-to-date runs/out (as a starter) shrunk with 270 pseudo-outs toward 0.155, × expected starter outs (26.6 − expected bullpen outs); opponent season-to-date runs per game (shrunk with 20 pseudo-games). Pseudo-counts chosen on 2025 by squared error.

Distribution on 2026: bullpen runs vs average sd 0.09 runs (10th–90th percentile -0.13 to +0.11); vs usual sd 0.04 (-0.04 to +0.06). Expected bullpen outs 11.28 (actual 11.46 incl. non-pool arms); expected bullpen runs 2.06 (actual 2.12, sd per game 2.36). Reliever rating sd across pool arms 0.0174 runs/out; 1.9% of pool arms have no relief history. Correlation of vs average with the naive team-rate deviation: 0.68.

## 6. Conclusions

* **Beyond season-average team quality** (naive → naive + vs average): ΔMSE +0.0165 ± 0.0090 (0.0107) season-forward, +0.0180 ± 0.0094 (0.0115) walk-forward. Slope on vs average given the naive rate: 2025 +1.39 ± 0.49, 2026 +1.61 ± 0.43 (clustered).
* **Availability alone** (naive → naive + vs usual): ΔMSE +0.0033 ± 0.0010 (0.0010); slope 2025 +0.35 ± 1.10, 2026 +3.48 ± 0.90.
* Pooled 2025-26 slopes (clustered): vs average given the naive rate +1.44 ± 0.37; vs usual given the naive rate +2.27 ± 0.66.
* Whole-game runs: adding vs average to starter + naive bullpen + opponent: ΔMSE +0.0548 ± 0.0198 (0.0190) season-forward, +0.0510 ± 0.0201 (0.0199) walk-forward; vs usual -0.0078 ± 0.0024 (0.0023), +0.0064 ± 0.0055 (0.0053).
* Per-game runs are very noisy (R² of any pre-game bullpen predictor is around 1%); effects are small relative to the game-to-game spread, so read slopes with their s.e.
