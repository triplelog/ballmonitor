# race_time: empirical uncertainty bands (weighted Riegel)

_Generated by `research_bands.py` — do not edit by hand._

Data: `data/backtest.csv` — 2560 train runners and 2440 test runners (one target race each, inputs strictly before the target; 1159 distinct target events in test). Everything below is fitted on train and scored once on test. Race weights: `race_weight_distance_scale` = 1, `race_weight_half_life_years` = 1 (chosen on train in RESEARCH_SIMPLE.md).

## 1. Riegel exponent (train)

Grid 1.00–1.12 (step 0.01), minimising train MAPE of the weighted Riegel point.

| runners | n | best b | train MAPE at best b | train MAPE at 1.06 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| all (gender not used) | 2560 | 1.07 | 10.176% | 10.206% |
| women | 1344 | 1.06 | 9.748% | 9.748% |
| men | 1216 | 1.09 | 10.542% | 10.713% |

Median log error ln(actual / point) × 100 by gender × direction (train; "up" = target longer than every input race, "down" = shorter; + = point too fast):

| exponent | F up | F down | M up | M down | RMS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| b = 1.06 for all | -0.74% | -0.07% | +2.14% | -2.36% | 1.64 |
| b = 1.07 for all | -1.48% | +0.84% | +1.41% | -1.50% | 1.34 |
| b = 1.06 women / 1.09 men | -0.74% | -0.07% | -0.30% | +0.25% | 0.42 |

Decision: the gender-specific exponent is simple (one offset per gender), lowers train MAPE (10.125% vs 10.176%) and cuts the gender × direction median errors (RMS 0.42 vs 1.34), so it is the default (`riegel_exponent` = 1.07 when gender is not given, offsets +0.02 men / -0.01 women).

## 2. Quantile model (train)

q(level) = base + gap·b_gap + years·b_years + b_races / races, in ln(actual / point); gap = weighted mean |ln(D/d)|, years = years since the most recent race (cap 3), races = number of races (cap 3).

| 97.5% | +0.2352 | +0.0628 | +0.0802 | +0.0003 | +0.3013 | +0.0585 |

Reading it: a single race (1/races = 1) mostly lowers the 2.5% quantile (more chance of running much faster than predicted — the one race may have been a jog); old races raise the upper quantiles (more chance of being slower); extrapolating in distance widens both tails. The median row is the bias correction (formula `median_time`).

## Caveats

- One state (Ohio), RunSignup-timed and uncertified courses, effort and weather unobserved (see RESEARCH.md); bands describe recreational road racers like these.
- Training inputs were at most 3 races and at most 3 years old, hence the caps; beyond the data (e.g. mile → marathon, gap > 2) the bands are linear extrapolations.
- Runners in the same target event share course and weather, so the 2,440 test cases are not fully independent (bootstrap resamples events).
- The course/weather multipliers (DRAFT) scale the bands but were not part of this fit.
