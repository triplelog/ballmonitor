# DAD v0.7.0 — which performance measure makes the best strength rating?

_Generated by `python -m ballmonitor.models.dad.research_strength`. Do not edit by hand._

## Question

DAD v0.6.0 answered two questions with one number: what a team accomplished
against its schedule (**résumé**) and how good it is (**strength**). RESEARCH.md
showed that as a strength measure its probit z-scale loses to a plain
schedule-adjusted margin rating. This study puts every candidate per-game
measure inside the same opponent-adjusted rating and asks which one best
predicts games it has not seen.

* Half-season test, **baseball** (6 league-seasons): second-half adjusted-margin RMSE vs the adjusted margin — DAD v0.7.0 1.000 [0.999, 1.000], DAD v0.6.0 0.990 [0.977, 1.007].

* No compression of big margins (cap, tanh, log) and no win bonus beat the raw
  margin by more than 0.1% on the development seasons, so the shipped transform
  is the raw margin in every family; shrinkage toward average is tuned per family.
* So the improved DAD measures deviations on the **margin scale**: game DAD =
  (margin − default team's expected spread) / σ, and the primary output is the
  team's shrunk opponent-adjusted strength minus the default team's rating, in σ
  per game (= points/goals/runs per game better than the default, ÷ σ).

## Data and design

Regular-season games from data/games_<league>.csv (fetch.py). Families: basketball
= NCAA men + women + NBA, football = FBS + NFL, baseball = MLB, soccer = Big-5.

| family | dev league-seasons | dev games scored | test league-seasons | test games scored |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| basketball | 14 | 47,536 | 17 | 55,187 |
| football | 11 | 3,835 | 10 | 3,778 |
| baseball | 5 | 10,949 | 6 | 13,165 |
| soccer | 25 | 7,800 | 30 | 9,264 |

* **Game level, walk-forward.** Within each league-season, ratings fitted on games
  before date t predict every game on date t; a game is scored once both teams
  have played at least this many earlier games (fixed a priori): mbb 5, wbb 5, nba 10, cfb 3, nfl 3, mlb 15, epl 5, laliga 5, bundesliga 5, seriea 5, ligue1 5.
* Rating for a candidate transform T: weighted least squares
  T(margin) = r_home − r_away + h·(not neutral), `ridge` phantom 0-margin games
  per team toward the league mean, optional recency half-life (days).
* Each predictor x (difference of the two teams' numbers) is mapped to outcomes
  with coefficients fitted on **earlier seasons of the same league** only:
  margin = b·x + c·home (OLS), P(home result) = logistic(a·x + d·home) with a
  draw counted as ½ (soft-label log loss and Brier). The first season of each
  league is therefore not scored. DAD v0.6.0 per game uses the same opponent
  ratings (ridge-1 adjusted margin before date t) and an average baseline.
* "DAD v0.7.0 as shipped" is the model's own formula: mean of (margin + opponent
  rating − home edge param) over the team's games, shrunk by `prior_games`, with
  opponent ratings from the benchmark rating before date t.
* Tuning on development seasons (<= 2020) only; test seasons > 2020.
  Intervals: 95% bootstrap over whole league-seasons (games within a
  league-season are not independent). Pooled RMSE ratios normalise each league
  by the benchmark's MSE so leagues on different scales pool fairly.

## What the numbers mean (baseline conversion)

* Strength rating S = Σ(margin + opponent rating − home edge) / (games + prior
  games): points/goals/runs per game against an average team on a neutral field.
* Strength vs default = S − R_default (the preset's rating, e.g. bubble).
* DAD per game = (S − R_default) / σ, σ = per-game margin noise: "how many
  game-to-game SDs better than the default team". Win chance vs default =
  Φ((S − R_default)/σ) (Skellam in soccer, Pythagorean in baseball).
* Résumé: Total DAD = Σ (margin − default's expected spread)/σ; WAD = Σ (result −
  P(default team wins that game)) = wins above the default.

## Caveats

* All differences between transforms are small (mostly < 0.5% of RMSE); game
  margins are dominated by noise. The gap to DAD v0.6.0 is larger in basketball
  and football (2–3%) than in baseball and soccer (< 0.5%), where low scoring
  makes the probit nearly linear.
* The grids, the 0.1% rule and the minimum-games thresholds were set by the
  author; the rule was written after seeing that all dev differences were tiny.
  Test seasons were not used for any choice.
* Opponent ratings come from the same games (before t); the model needs them as
  inputs. College baseball/softball are untested (no complete public data).
* MLB's home edge is measured on recorded margins (home team often does not bat
  in the 9th).
