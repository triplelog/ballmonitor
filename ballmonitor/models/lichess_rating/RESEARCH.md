# lichess_rating: walk-forward study

_Generated by `research.py` — do not edit by hand._

Data: the Lichess open database (database.lichess.org, CC0), every rated standard game 2014-01 – 2017-08 (211,472,649 games), reduced by `download.py` to per-player daily totals for a fixed 1-in-4 sample of players (hash of the name). `pipeline.py` turns them into 8,361,353 cases (307,926 players; blitz, bullet and classical) — one per player, speed, monthly cutoff and horizon (30/91/182/365 days) for players who played that speed in the 91 days before the cutoff.

Split: **train** = half the players (hash), cases whose target date is before 2016-04-01 (cutoffs 2014-07-01 – 2016-03-01); **test** = the other half, cutoffs from 2016-04-01 (to 2017-08-01). Fitted on blitz train cases only; 60,000 random test cases scored. Test targets span about 17 months, so the 365-day results rest on about one independent year; cases of one player at different cutoffs overlap and are not independent.

This uses the future, so it is not a forecast input. It is also not proof that playing more causes improvement: players who are improving may simply play more.
