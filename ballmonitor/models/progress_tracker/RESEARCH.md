# Progress tracker: research

_Generated by `python -m ballmonitor.models.progress_tracker.research`; do not edit._

Question: when someone logs a measure every week or so and fits a straight line through the last 8 weeks, how much of that pace is still there 1, 3, 6 and 12 months later? The tracker shows two lines: **keep this pace** (the straight line, what the owner asked for) and **likely**, where the weekly progress halves every *half-life* weeks. This study picks the half-life and the width of the range for each kind of measure, on real logs.

| strength | fit20 studio records 2010-2016, member x machine (Steele et al. 2022, CC BY 4.0) | 65,294 | 1,978,873 | load of the best set that day (kg) | log |
| running | BMClab Strava weekly totals 2019-2020 (Afonseca et al. 2022, CC BY 4.0) | 36,404 | 2,748,054 | week's average pace (s/km) | log |

A case is a series on the 1st of a month with at least 3 entries in the last 8 weeks spanning at least 2 weeks (recency half-life 0 = equal weights). The fit is the model's own (weighted least squares on the value, or its log, against weeks). The actual is the entry nearest the target date within 7 days (4 weeks), 14 days (13 weeks), 14 days (26 weeks), 14 days (52 weeks).

**People who stopped are excluded.** A case only counts at a horizon if the person logged an entry near that date, so everything below is *for people who kept going*. Share of observable cases with no entry near the target (stopped or paused):

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| chess | 33% of 137,351 | 36% of 112,954 | 43% of 77,754 | 53% of 29,478 |
| strength | 21% of 154,262 | 20% of 132,306 | 31% of 101,212 | 44% of 41,623 |
| running | 9% of 169,119 | 7% of 124,480 | 9% of 77,352 | - |

**Splits.** People are split in half by md5 of their id. chess: train = train people with targets before 2016-04-01, test = test people with cutoffs from then to the end of the data. strength: the same with 2015-07-01. running: only 2019-2020 exist, so it is split by people only (train and test share the same calendar weeks), and targets after 2020-03-10 are dropped (COVID lockdowns).

**Independent periods.** Cases overlap: each person appears at many cutoffs and all horizons, and everyone shares one calendar. Non-overlapping target windows in the test periods:

| kind | test cutoffs | 4 wk | 13 wk | 26 wk | 52 wk | test people |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| chess | 2016-04-01 .. 2017-07-01 | 17 | 5 | 2 | 1 | 21,048 |
| strength | 2015-07-01 .. 2016-11-01 | 18 | 6 | 3 | 1 | 17,164 |
| running | 2019-04-01 .. 2020-02-01 | 11 | 3 | 1 | 0 | 17,434 |

So the 52-week results rest on about one or two independent years per kind, and the running test shares its calendar with training. Bootstrap intervals resample people and ignore the shared calendar, so they are too narrow.

## Body weight and body fat

No open dataset of repeated body-weight or body-fat logs was available, so their half-life is a judgement (v0.1.0 config `half_life_weeks_body`, marked DRAFT), and their **likely line and range were not tested**. Reasoning: for a fixed change in calories, weight changes at a rate that shrinks as the body adapts, with a time constant of about a year (Hall et al. 2011, Lancet 378:826-837: about half the eventual change after one year); real diets also drift, so the half-life is a guess at the slow end.

## How the model uses this

Model v0.2.0 no longer forecasts from the rolling line: it tracks a plan the person sets (even, faster early or faster late) and scales it by the share of the planned progress made so far. That prediction is motivational and untested; this study is the background: short-term pace does not persist, and newcomers' gains come mostly early, which is why 'faster early' is the realistic plan shape.
