# Running progress: research

_Generated by `python -m ballmonitor.models.running_progress.research --write-config`; do not edit._

## Data

BMClab weekly training totals (Afonseca, Watanabe & Duarte 2022, PeerJ 10:e13192; data doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.16620238.v5, CC BY 4.0): 36,412 Strava users who had run a World Marathon Major, 104 weeks from 2019-01-01 (each year binned in 52 weeks from 1 January, so 31 December is not in the weekly files). Columns used: weekly distance (km) and duration (min); gender, age group and country for checks. The dataset has no heart rate and no race results.

- 76% men; age groups {'35 - 54': 0.593, '18 - 34': 0.336, '55 +': 0.071}; top countries {'United States': 13748, 'United Kingdom': 7565, 'Germany': 2138, 'Canada': 1144, 'France': 903}.
- Weeks with no running: 24.7% in 2019, 29.7% in 2020.
- Cleaning: of 2,756,863 run weeks, 8,809 (0.32%) are treated as missing: 595 faster than 3:00/km, 8,061 slower than 15:00/km, 160 over 300 km.
- Southern-hemisphere athletes (seasons shifted 6 months): 1,693.

## Cases and splits

A cutoff ('today') every 2 weeks from week 12, horizons (4, 6, 9, 13, 19, 26) weeks; a case is an athlete with at least 3 run weeks in the 12 weeks before the cutoff. Features use only weeks before the cutoff (up to 26); the outcome is the 4 weeks ending at the target date. Athletes are split by md5(id): 21,773 train, 14,639 test. Cases were subsampled per cutoff and horizon (shares {'train': 0.35, 'new19': 0.15, 'pre2020': 1.0, 'covid': 0.15}).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| train | train athletes | ending in 2019 | 583,171 | 542,254 |
| new19 | test athletes | ending in 2019 | 167,941 | 156,114 |
| pre2020 | test athletes | 2020-01-01 .. 2020-03-10 | 269,306 | 247,888 |

**Independent periods.** Cases overlap heavily: one athlete appears at many cutoffs and horizons, and every athlete shares the same calendar. The fit sees one year (2019; about 13 non-overlapping 4-week target windows, one seasonal cycle). The honest test, **pre2020**, covers about **2.5 non-overlapping 4-week windows** (January to early March 2020) of new athletes, so it says little about other seasons. **new19** is out of sample in people but not in time (same weeks as the fit). **covid** is out of sample in people and time but the lockdowns changed training (Afonseca et al. report 7.5% less distance in 2020), so it is reported separately and not used to choose anything. Intervals below resample athletes (cluster bootstrap), which ignores the calendar dependence and so are too narrow.

## What to predict, and why

- **Average pace over the 4 weeks before the target date** (total time / total distance; primary). It is the only fitness signal in the data. It mixes easy runs, workouts and races, so it is a loose fitness proxy: a runner who adds easy volume gets slower on this measure while getting fitter. Four weeks smooth out single runs; the forecast is conditional on still training.
- **Mean weekly km over the same 4 weeks**, given still training (rest weeks count as 0).
- **The chance of still training**: at least one run logged in those 4 weeks.
- **Implied marathon time (Tanda 2011)**: from the paper only, not validated here (no races).

The starting point is the 12-week pace, not the last 4 weeks: as a forecast it is better at every horizon (baselines below). Days with a run would be a natural consistency measure, but the daily files (~309 MB) were not needed: everything is computed from weekly totals so a weekly log works as well as a daily one.

## Things tried that did not help much (negative results)

### pace on pre2020: extra terms

|  | cases | MAPE % | median APE % | median bias % | in 50% | in 80% | in 95% | pinball x100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| model (default) | 247,888 | 5.70 | 3.53 | -0.18 | 0.541 | 0.834 | 0.961 | 1.755 |
| model + Banister fitness/fatigue | 247,888 | 5.68 | 3.50 | -0.16 | 0.543 | 0.835 | 0.962 | 1.749 |
| model + gender/age | 247,888 | 5.67 | 3.51 | -0.23 | 0.543 | 0.835 | 0.962 | 1.744 |

- **Banister impulse-response.** Fitness and fatigue as exponentially weighted weekly minutes (time constants 6 and 1 weeks, i.e. 42 and 7 days; training load = minutes, as there is no heart rate for TRIMP). Adding ln(fatigue/fitness) (-0.0055) and ln fitness (-0.0021) to the pace location improves the pre2020 pinball loss by 0.3%: with weekly totals and a pace that mixes easy and hard running, the fitness-fatigue balance adds almost nothing to the pace trends. Not in the model.
- **Gender and age group.** Men -0.0078, 18-34 -0.0031, 55+ +0.0051 (log pace change); pinball gain 0.6%. Not worth three more inputs, so they are accepted only for display. The by-group table shows that women and the 55+ group ran slightly slower than predicted (bias = median / actual - 1, so negative = predicted too fast) and that the bands are too wide for women.
- **Season.** Pace: dropping it costs 0.9% pinball on pre2020 and 0.6% on covid. Weekly km: the season terms help on pre2020 (0.9% pinball) but hurt on covid (+1.9%), where lockdowns cancelled the spring marathons the 2019 curve expects. Kept, with that caveat.

## Tanda (2011) implied marathon time

Tanda's formula Pm (s/km) = 17.1 + 140 exp(-0.0053 K) + 0.55 P from mean weekly km K and mean training pace P over the 8 weeks before a race was fitted on 22 runners (46 marathons, 2:47-3:36). Tanda & Knechtle (2013) found a standard error of 28 min when applying it to 126 recreational runners. This dataset has no race results, so the implied times are shown as a literature translation only. On the pre2020 cases (today's 8 weeks):

- median implied marathon 3:44; 10-90%: 3:14 to 4:22;
- 35% fall inside the paper's 2:47-3:36 range (the rest are extrapolation).
