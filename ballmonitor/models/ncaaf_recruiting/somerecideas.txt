How can we evaluate the quality of a recruiting class after they are finished playing? We don't want to reward a class for a team's on-field success if no one in that class ever contributed. For baseball, WAR does a good job of establishing each player's contribution. 

The basic idea is that the credit for a victory will be distributed amongst the players that played the most important roles. Skill players are relatively easy to quantify. For defensive players, tackles offer a rough approximation of contribution. For the offensive line, games played is the best we can do. Other data such as end-of-season awards and draft position will be included. 

Blame for losses will be distributed equally amongst all recruits. Someone on the bench shouldn't get credit for a win, but a loss is somewhat their fault for not being good enough to contribute. 

The details of how to convert less than perfect data into a ranking is never going to be perfect but should be better than just using wins over the next 4 years. Aggregating over an entire recruiting class should smooth out some of the noise.

Value of win. % to offense. % to defense. % to OL, QB, WR, RB, TE. % to DL, LB, DB. Divide amongst players by contribution.

Value of loss. % to offense. % to defense. % to OL, QB, WR, RB, TE. % to DL, LB, DB. Divide amongst players equally (or by year).
